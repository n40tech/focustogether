# Focus Together — game plan

A calm, sound-first study game for a small group of friends. You "go to work"
together: you study at your desk in a shared office, earn poker chips for every
minute you focus, and spend your breaks in a shared allotment garden where you
can chat, smoke and tend your crops.

This is a living document. The **Decisions** section records what has been
agreed; **Open questions** lists what hasn't.

---

## 1. Design pillars

1. **One place at a time.** The car park, the office and the garden are separate
   full screens. While a session runs, the office is the only place that exists.
2. **Seen from above and in front.** Every place is drawn top-front, like a
   tabletop diorama: you see the ground, and the tops and fronts of things. There
   is no sky. The time of day shows only as the scene's changing hues, and the
   weather shows on the ground.
3. **Calm over clever.** Muted palettes, slow motion, no badges, feeds, red dots
   or confetti. The timer is the only number that changes while you study.
4. **Sound is the world.** The shared weather is something you *hear*: rain on
   the office window, wind through the allotments, birds when it clears.
5. **Presence, not pressure.** You see friends' cars, desks and avatars. There
   are no leaderboards and nobody else's chip count.
6. **Community first, you second.** You're never centre stage: your desk is one
   of twelve identical desks, your car takes a bay like anyone else's, and your
   allotment is one plot in a shared grid.

## 3. Places

### Car park (where the app opens)
- **See:** the office building and its door, three even rows of four bays and
  a bike rack. Bays and rack slots follow desks: whoever has desk 5 parks in
  bay 5 (or rack slot 5). Friends who are *at work* (arrived, studying, on a
  break, or between the two) appear as their **car** or **bike**; friends with
  neither aren't shown. Nobody's bay is reserved, yours included.
- **Do:** walk in (office) · the noticeboard (friends and invites) · the garden
  gate (only while you're on a break) · go home (while you're at work) · log out.
- **Hear:** open-air weather, distant traffic.

### Office
- **See:** twelve identical desks in four rows of three. When you walk in you
  get a random desk that none of your friends at work is using, and it stays
  yours (across breaks) until you go home. Friends appear at their desks as
  they arrive: sitting with their back to you and their monitor glowing while
  they study, an empty chair otherwise. You sit at yours while you're in the
  office, among them rather than in front. Everyone's desk shows what they've
  bought for it. Frosted windows with blinds let the light in, and rain runs
  down the glass, but they show no sky.
- **Do, before a session:** choose a length (1–120 min) and start, or walk out.
- **Do, during a session:** nothing else. There's the countdown and a small
  *give up* link, which forfeits the session. The chip balance, shop and all
  navigation are hidden. **Stay on the page**: on a phone, switch apps or
  tabs, or lock it, for more than 10 seconds and the session stops with
  nothing paid. On a computer, other tabs and windows are fine, but closing
  the page stops it (see *Staying on the page*). The screen is kept awake
  meanwhile.
  A new player can **skip their first session**, to see the payout and the
  garden before they've studied.
- **Hear:** a soft room tone, the weather muffled through the window, rain
  tapping on the glass.

### Payout (end of a session)
- **See:** a closer look at your own desk (the same desk as in the office, monitor
  and all, at the same proportions), with chips counted out onto it, and the
  break you've earned. Neighbouring desks show at the edges on wide screens.
- **Do:** *Take a break* (garden) · *Go home* (car park) · *Spend chips* (shed).
- **Hear:** a soft chime, then chips clinking.

### Garden (on a break)
- **See:** a garden three phones wide. Everyone's allotment sits on one 7 × 3
  grid by a slot handed out when they joined, so a plot is always in the same
  place for everyone who can see it; friends' plots appear as you add them.
  Along the front: a gravel path, the shed, a little market square with trestle
  tables, two benches, two wheelie bins, a noticeboard, a post box and the
  gate. Friends in the garden walk about too, with
  Club Penguin-style chat bubbles over their heads, smoke if they're having a
  cigarette and a basket if they've something on their stall. A quiet break
  timer.
- **Do:** walk with the thumbstick (on the right, under your thumb; or the
  arrow keys / WASD) · say something. What else you can do depends on where
  you're standing: plant, look at or harvest your beds and water them; water
  a friend's beds; go into the shed; buy from a friend's stall, or offer them
  a swap, when you're next to them; sit on a bench (friends see you sitting;
  walk off to get up); throw things in a bin; the noticeboard (friends and
  invites, as in the car park); the post box (letters). With a friend beside
  you there, you get both: the place's button and theirs. Walking out through
  the gate takes you back to work. From anywhere: smoke · your basket (eat,
  save seeds, sell, offer, and answer offers) · your stall · *Back to work*
  (office) · *Go home*.
- **Hear:** the weather in full, birds when it's dry, crickets at night, wind
  chimes when it gusts, and footsteps (yours, and friends' from their side of
  you) on grass and gravel.
- When the break runs out, a bell rings and you choose the office or home.

### Shed (the shop)
Reached from the shed door in the garden and from the payout screen. It's
never available during a session.

### Noticeboard (friends and invites)
See where everyone is, add a friend by username, unpin a friend, and make
invites. Only invites still waiting to be used are listed: once someone joins,
they're on the board as a friend. There's one in the car park and one in the
garden. It also brings back the tips for new players.

Unpinning asks first, then ends the friendship both ways (as adding makes
it): you stop seeing each other, and any offers between you are called off,
with what was offered handed back. Letters already sent stay. Nobody is told;
they just drop off each other's board, and either can add the other again.

## 4. Rules

### Status
Derived on the server from timestamps and never stored, so it can't drift.

| Status | When | Car park | Office | Garden |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `focused` | a session is running | vehicle shown | at their desk | – |
| `on_break` | finished a session less than their earned break ago, and hasn't gone home | vehicle shown | at their desk until they get up, then an empty chair | walking about, if they have the garden open |
| `idle` | arrived, or a break (or a given-up or stopped session) ended, less than 15 min ago; or their app is open at their desk or in the garden | vehicle shown | at their desk until they get up, then an empty chair | – |
| `absent` | otherwise, or they went home | – | – (desk given up) | – |

A desk (and with it a bay and a rack slot) is held for as long as you're not
`absent`. Where you are at work is kept too (`profiles.place`): walking into
the office sits you at your desk, and you stay there, for friends to see,
through the end of a session and the payout, until you get up and go to the
garden, walk out to the car park, or go home. While your app is open at your
desk or in the garden (it checks in every few seconds), you count as at work
however long ago your break ended; close it and the usual timings apply.

### Sessions, chips and breaks
- Sessions last 1–120 minutes. Completing one earns **about 1 chip per
  minute**: the minutes, give or take a little luck (up to 12% either way,
  usually much less; at least 1 chip). The payout says when luck added or
  took some. Giving up earns nothing, and no break.
- **Earned break: 1 minute per 5 studied** (at least 1). So 25 min earns 5,
  50 earns 10, 90 earns 18 and 120 earns 24.
- When the break runs out, a soft bell rings and you choose office or home.
  Starting a new session ends a break early; going home ends it too, and so
  does logging out (it counts as going home).

### Staying on the page
- A session belongs to the page (browser tab) it was started on. On a phone
  or tablet, if that page is hidden (another app, another tab, the phone
  locked) for **more than 10 seconds**, the session stops at the moment you
  left: no chips, no break. Back within 10 seconds, it carries on, so a slip
  of the thumb is forgiven.
- On a computer (one with a mouse or trackpad), **other tabs and windows are
  fine**, for lecture notes and reading. Only leaving the page itself counts:
  closing its tab, or going to another site in it, with the same 10 seconds'
  grace, so a reload is forgiven.
- The page tells the server the moment it's left (a request the browser
  finishes even as the page goes), and on coming back says how long it was
  away, in case the first word never got through (no signal, say). If the
  session had finished meanwhile, without you, it's taken back, chips and all.
- Only the page you started on counts: a phone left open on Focus Together
  beside the laptop you're studying on can't stop your session. The screen is kept awake
  during a session (where the browser allows), so it doesn't lock by itself.
- The office says when a session stopped because you left, until you start
  another.

### A first day
- **Tips** guide a new player, one at a time, at the top of the panel: walk
  in, pick a length and start, stay on the page, walk about the garden, buy
  seeds, plant, water, write to a friend. After their first studied session
  (not a skipped one), the car park and the garden suggest making an invite on
  the noticeboard. Each goes once it's done or put away (*Got it*), and is
  remembered per account (`profiles.tips`); the noticeboard brings them all
  back.
- **On a phone's browser**, a new player's first tip in the car park says how
  to add Focus Together to the home screen, with the steps for their phone:
  Safari's on an iPhone (and to sign in again from there, as the home-screen
  app keeps its own sign-in), Chrome's on Android. When Chrome offers to
  install it, that's a single *Add*. Opened from the home screen, it's not
  shown.
- **The first session can be skipped** once it's started: it ends at once, as
  a session of the length chosen (25 minutes at most), with its payout and
  break, so there's something to spend in the garden. Only ever the first.

### Shop catalogue (placeholder prices, tuned to ~1 chip per minute)

| Kind | Items (chips) |
|---|---|
| Vehicles | Bicycle 60 · Hatchback 300 · Estate 500 · Campervan 800 · Vintage roadster 1200 |
| Desk | Mug 10 · Rubber duck 15 · Succulent 20 · Photo frame 30 · Desk lamp 40 · Lava lamp 60 · Radio 80 · Bonsai 150 |
| Seeds (packet of 3) | Radish 6 · Carrot 8 · Sunflower 10 · Strawberry 12 · Tomato 15 · Pumpkin 25 |
| Cigarettes | Pack of 5 · 8 |

Vehicles and desk items are one-offs. Buying a vehicle makes it the one you
arrive in. The catalogue is one table (`items`), so prices are tuned without
touching code.

### Allotment
- Every player has a plot with **4 beds**, and a bed holds **4 plants**.
- **One seed makes one plant**, in the next free spot of a bed; plants in a
  bed can be different crops, and each grows on its own clock. **Crops grow in
  real time**, studying or not:
  from a day for radishes to five for a pumpkin. A watering lasts a day, and
  meanwhile the bed grows **half as fast again**; nothing dies of thirst, it
  just grows at its own pace. When it's raining and you're in the garden, the
  rain waters your beds for you. A bed's sheet says when it'll be ripe.
- **A ripe crop keeps for 2 days** after it ripens. In its last 12 hours it
  starts to go over (yellowing leaves, dull
  fruit), then it **withers**: it gives nothing, and stays in its bed until
  you clear it onto the compost. The payout and the car park mention what's
  ripe and how long it keeps, and friends can see a withered plot.
- Picking a bed takes everything ripe in it into your basket as produce (a
  radish or carrot plant gives one root; see the table). Clearing a bed pulls
  up only what has withered. **Produce is never exchanged for chips by the
  game.** You can:
  - **eat** it (a munching sound, nothing more),
  - **save seeds** from it (one crop gives 2–6 seeds of its kind),
  - **sell** it to friends at the market, or **trade** it,
  - or **throw it away** in a bin (seeds and cigarettes too).
- You can **water a friend's beds** (standing next to their plot), but only
  they can plant and harvest them.

| Crop | Ripe after (never watered) | Kept watered | Picked per plant | Seeds saved per crop |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Radish | 1 day | 16 hours | 1 | 2 |
| Carrot | 1.5 days | 1 day | 1 | 2 |
| Strawberry | 2 days | 32 hours | 4 | 2 |
| Tomato | 3 days | 2 days | 4 | 3 |
| Sunflower | 4 days | 64 hours | 1 | 6 |
| Pumpkin | 5 days | 80 hours | 1 | 4 |

### Market
- Put produce, seeds or cigarettes on **your stall** at a price per item
  (1–999 chips; up to 12 different things). What's on the stall leaves your
  basket until it sells or you take it back.
- A friend **standing next to you** in the garden can buy from it, one or all;
  the chips go straight to you. Both of you have to be on a break, and you
  have to be friends (the server checks both; the client checks you're close).
- The market tables show what's on the stalls of whoever's in the garden.

### Trades (swaps and gifts)
- Offer some produce, seeds or cigarettes to a friend, optionally asking for
  something back. Asking for nothing makes it a gift.
- The offered items are held in escrow until your friend accepts or declines,
  or you withdraw the offer, so nobody can be short-changed.
- Offers are made and answered in the garden (from your basket, or face to
  face), never during a session.

### Invites
- Sign-up is invite-only. Invites are 8-character codes (no look-alike
  characters) that last 14 days; you can hold 5 unused ones at a time.
- Whoever joins with your invite starts out as your friend. Friendships stay
  pairwise: you see your friends, not your friends' friends.

### Cigarettes
Smoked in the garden, one at a time. Each burns for **3 minutes**, getting
shorter as it goes, then it's out. Friends see the smoke and hear a lighter
flick and a quiet crackle.

### Post
- The **post box** in the garden takes letters (up to 1000 characters) to
  friends. A letter waits in the post box until its friend next opens it
  there; the car park mentions waiting letters, and an envelope shows in the
  slot. Sent letters say whether they've been picked up.
- Letters are only readable by whoever wrote them and whoever they're for.

### Bins
Two wheelie bins in the garden take produce, seeds and cigarettes you don't
want. Nothing comes back out.

### Chat
Messages of up to 80 characters appear as a bubble over your head for a few
seconds, Club Penguin style. Only friends who are in the garden at that moment
see them, and nothing is stored.

### Walking about
Positions aren't stored either. While you walk, your position goes out on
your garden channel about 5 times a second, and friends' screens ease between
updates. You say hello when you arrive (friends reply with where they are),
bye when you leave, and where you are every 20 seconds while you stand still;
a friend not heard from for 50 seconds has gone.

### On two devices at once
You can be in the garden on a phone and a laptop at once, and you're in one
place on both: your devices listen on your own channel too. Steer on either
and the other follows; a device that's just arrived takes the position the
others have, rather than starting at the gate. Walking out through the gate on
one takes both to the office. What you say shows on both.

Steer on both at once and they can disagree. Walking the same way they stay
together, but once they're 96 units apart (the garden is 1080 across) a
**rift** opens at the midpoint between them, with a low rushing sound. You
vanish into it, and a moment later both devices land in the car park, with a
note saying why. The break carries on. Friends in the garden see the rift
open where you were, and close again a few seconds later.

## 5. Weather

- One **shared, deterministic** weather cycle computed from the clock.
  Everyone sees and hears the same weather at the same moment, with no server or
  API involved.
- Smooth value noise on several time scales (hours, ~30 min, ~10 min) produces
  `cloud`, `rain`, `wind`, `storm` and `fog`, each from 0 to 1. Rain only falls
  under heavy cloud, storms need heavy rain and strong wind, and fog settles on
  still mornings.
- Over a year it comes out roughly Bristol-like: clear about 38% of the time,
  cloudy or overcast 35%, wet 23% (drizzle, rain, heavy rain and the odd
  thunderstorm), and morning mist 3%. Rain spells last about an hour.
- Lightning strikes fall at deterministic moments too, so everyone in the
  garden sees the flash and hears the thunder together, later the further
  away it is.
- With no sky in view, weather shows on the ground: rain slanting with the
  wind, ripples, wet ground that dries out over the following hour, cloud
  shadows drifting across, mist, and leaves blown through in gusts.
- Time of day (the colour cast over each scene, birds or crickets) follows
  each player's own clock and a rough Bristol sunrise and sunset for the date.
- It lives in `src/weather/`. The same function can be ported to SQL or an Edge
  Function if the server ever needs it, for example so rain waters plots while
  nobody is looking.

## 6. Sound

Procedural for now (Web Audio API, no audio files). Each layer is driven by a
weather or scene parameter, so recorded loops can replace layers one at a time
later.

| Layer | Driven by | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Rain hiss and drops | `rain` | outdoors; muffled, plus taps on the glass, indoors |
| Wind and gusts | `wind` | outdoors; faint indoors |
| Thunder | `storm` | everywhere, muffled indoors |
| Birds / crickets | dry daylight / dry night | garden, car park |
| Wind chimes | gusts | garden |
| Traffic rumble | — | car park |
| Room tone | — | office |

- One-shots: door, bell, chips, lighter, watering can, bubble pop, harvest,
  purchase, munching, and footsteps on grass and gravel (friends' quieter
  with distance and panned to their side).
- Scenes crossfade over about 3 s. Indoors, the weather passes through a
  low-pass "window" filter. Weather changes ramp over about 10 s, so the sound
  never jumps.
- Browsers only allow audio after a tap; the first tap anywhere unlocks it.
  Mute and volume are remembered per device.
- On native (later), either `react-native-audio-api` (a Web Audio
  implementation, so the same engine runs) or `expo-audio` with recorded loops.

## 7. Visual calmness rules

- Each place is a full-screen scene with its own muted palette. Scenes fade in;
  they never slide.
- Time of day is a single colour cast over the whole scene: rose at dawn,
  neutral by day, amber in the golden hour, violet at dusk, deep blue at night.
  Lamps, lit windows and screens are drawn above the cast, so they glow after
  dark. UI panels switch from warm paper to dim slate at night.
- No scrolling feeds, badges, red dots or confetti. The chip balance is hidden
  while studying.
- Motion is slow and ambient: rain, drifting clouds, smoke. Lightning is a
  gentle brightening, never a strobe. With *reduce motion* turned on, the
  weather is drawn still.
- UI chrome is a thin header (place, chips, sound) and a bottom action panel.
  Errors and notes appear as a short toast under the header and fade away.
- Placeholder art is built from simple shapes on a fixed 360×640 "stage" that
  scales to fit between the header and the bottom panel. Backdrops (walls,
  roads, hedges, spare beds and trees) run past the stage edges, so wide
  screens look like a wider diorama rather than a phone in the middle. Real
  illustrations can replace pieces later without changing scene layout code.

## 8. Architecture

```
src/
  app/                    routes: one file per place (Expo Router)
    _layout.tsx           auth gate
    login.tsx
    (app)/_layout.tsx     world state + the "studying locks you in the office" rule
    (app)/index.tsx       car park
    (app)/office.tsx      office + timer
    (app)/payout.tsx      end of session
    (app)/garden.tsx      garden
    (app)/shop.tsx        shed (shop)
    (app)/friends.tsx     noticeboard
  world/                  client game state: polling and the payout prompt (World),
                          who sits where (seats), the garden's layout (gardenMap),
                          walking (useWalker), the garden channel (useGardenChannel)
  weather/                shared weather algorithm + scene lighting
  audio/                  sound engine: engine.web.ts (Web Audio), engine.ts (native stub)
  art/                    placeholder art: the scene stage and its light, weather,
                          vehicles, desks, people, beds
  components/             UI pieces: buttons, header, toast, timer, thumbstick, sheets
  lib/                    Supabase client, typed RPC wrappers, catalogue art, time,
                          leaving the page mid-session (away)
supabase/migrations/      0001 init (MVP), 0002 game, 0003 community, 0004 withering,
                          0005 testing tools, 0006 real-time growth and desks,
                          0007 staying on the page, a first day, post and bins
supabase/tests/           scenario tests for the game rules (plain Postgres + stubs)
e2e/                      browser tests: the web build clicked through in Chromium,
                          against a pretend Supabase (Playwright)
deployment/               numbered runbooks for setting up and shipping each update
```

- **The server is the source of truth** for anything that matters: chips,
  status, inventory, plots and trades. Clients never write tables. Every change
  is a security-definer Postgres function using `auth.uid()`, and RLS only
  allows reads.
- **Polling:** the world polls `get_me` and `get_friends_board` every 10 s and
  when the app regains focus (back on the page mid-session, the first poll is
  `back_to_page` instead). The garden polls `get_garden` every 5 s. A poll that
  comes back after an action's own answer (going home, say) is dropped, so it
  can't undo the action.
- **Garden channel:** Supabase Realtime Broadcast on private channels named
  `garden:<username>`, carrying chat, positions and rifts. RLS on
  `realtime.messages` lets you listen to your own and your friends' channels,
  and speak only on your own. Your own channel is how your devices keep
  together.
- **Seats:** the server hands out desks so friends at work never share one.
  Two of your friends who aren't friends with each other can hold the same
  number, though; then the later arrival moves along to the next free desk on
  your screen. Plot slots past the 21 cells of the grid wrap round the same way.
- **Clock:** every RPC returns `server_now` and the client corrects its
  countdowns with it.
- **Routing:** while a session runs, every place except the office is removed
  from the navigator (`Stack.Protected`), so deep links and the back button
  can't escape the office.
- **Scene layers**, bottom to top: the art (in stage units), weather on the
  ground, the time-of-day colour cast, lights that glow through it, untinted
  name tags and chat bubbles, then the UI.
- **Previewing:** on web, `?weather=clear|cloudy|windy|rain|storm|mist` and
  `?hour=0-23` change what you see and hear (and nothing else).

## 9. Data model and API

| Table | Holds |
|---|---|

| `sessions` | + `chips`: what a completed session paid (0003); + `device`: the page it was started on, and `left_at`: when that page was left (0007) |
| `friendships` | unchanged; `remove_friend` deletes both rows (0008) |
| `items` | catalogue: slug, kind, name, price, pack size |
| `crops` | per crop: its seed, study minutes to ripen, harvest size, seeds saved |
| `inventory` | (player, item) → quantity |

| `crops` | + `grow_hours` (0006): hours to ripen unwatered |
| `trades` | offers in escrow: what's given, what's wanted, status |
| `listings` | market stalls: seller, item, quantity, price per item (0003) |

| `list_item(item, qty, price)`, `unlist(id)` | on a break | garden |
| `buy_listing(id, qty)` | both on a break, and friends | garden |
| `create_invite()`, `get_invites()` | signed in | invite / invites |
| `check_signup(code, username)` | anyone, signed in or not | whether that sign-up would work, and whose invite it is |

Sign-up itself is Supabase Auth's: a trigger on `auth.users` (0003) refuses
any account without a valid invite in its metadata, then makes the profile,
uses up the invite and befriends whoever made it.

Usernames (0008) are 3 to 20 letters, numbers or `_`, stored in lower case.
Letters can have accents (josé, zoë, straße). Supabase Auth only takes plain
emails, so an account's email is the username's *plain spelling*: accents
off, and ß, æ, ø and a few others spelt out (`zoe_strasse@focus.test`). No
two players share a plain spelling, so "jose" and "josé" can't both exist,
and you can sign in or add a friend by either. The rules are
`private.is_username` and `private.username_key`, mirrored in
`src/lib/username.ts`.

## 12. Decisions

| Question | Decision |
|---|---|
| Where does the weather come from? | A shared, procedural cycle seeded by the clock: the same for everyone. |
| How does the allotment grow? | In real time over a few days, depending on the crop (it used to be by the minutes studied); watering speeds it up. Crops are never sold to the game for chips; they're eaten, sold to friends, traded or saved for seeds. |
| How do friends without a car appear in the car park? | They don't. You can buy a bike (shown in the bike rack) or a car. |
| How does a break end? | An earned break timer (1 min per 5 studied), then a bell and a choice of office or home. |
| Perspective | Top-front for every place, with no sky: time of day only shows as the scene's changing hues. |
| Web hosting | EAS Hosting with a static export; Supabase for the backend. |
| Social model | Pairwise friendships, as in the MVP. |
| Un-adding a friend | "Unpin" on the noticeboard, after a question. It ends the friendship both ways and calls off offers between you; nobody is told. |
| Usernames with accents | Allowed (Latin letters). The plain spelling is unique, so accents never tell two players apart. |
| Eating | Just a munching sound. Nothing to see, no effect. |
| Friends' plots | You can water them, but not plant or harvest. |
| Sign-ups | Invite-only for now. Whoever invites you starts as your friend. |
| "Roughly" 1 chip a minute | The minutes, plus or minus a little random luck (up to 12%, usually far less). |
| Where you sit | Not at the front: the office starts as twelve empty desks, and everyone (you included) gets a random one on arrival, kept across breaks until they go home. |
| Car park | No kiosk; three even rows of bays that follow the desks. |
| The garden | Walkable with a thumbstick, three phones wide and one tall, with every allotment on one grid in a fixed place. |
| Trading | A market: stalls with prices, bought from face to face in the garden. Swaps and gifts by offer stay too. |
| Unpicked crops | Wither 2 days after ripening (going over for the last 12 hours), give nothing, and must be cleared before replanting. |
| After a session | You stay at your desk (friends see you there) until you choose to leave it. |
| Testing | A Test button, only in local builds with `EXPO_PUBLIC_TESTING=true` and only for accounts marked as testers. |
| Leaving the page mid-session | Stops the session with nothing paid, after 10 seconds' grace. Only the page the session started on counts. |
| Other tabs on a computer | Fine, for lecture notes: on a computer only closing the page counts as leaving. On a phone or tablet, another app or tab still does. |
| A friend standing at a place | Doesn't hide it: you get the place's button and the friend's (by name). |
| New players | Tips, one at a time, and a skippable first session (as 25 minutes at most). |
| Planting | One seed, one visible plant; four plants to a bed, any mix of crops. |

## 13. Open questions

1. **Overlapping friend circles:** with pairwise friendships, two of your
   friends who aren't friends with each other can hold the same desk number.
   Each viewer then sees one of them moved along a desk, so people can sit in
   slightly different places on different screens. Rare in a friend group;
   shared "offices" (groups) would remove it.
2. **Swaps by offer:** keep asynchronous offers alongside the face-to-face
   market, or make all trading face to face?
3. **Thumbstick on computers:** keep it on screen when there's a keyboard, or
   hide it after the first arrow-key press?
4. **Market prices:** leave them entirely to players, or suggest a price per
   item?

## 14. Risks

- **App store review:** tobacco items affect age ratings on the App Store and
  Google Play, and Apple rejects apps that encourage tobacco use. Cigarettes
  are one row in `items`, so they can be renamed or swapped per platform.
- **Audio autoplay:** browsers stay silent until the first tap. The car park's
  first interaction unlocks sound.
- **Studying on a computer is on trust:** other tabs are allowed (for lecture
  notes), so Focus Together can't tell notes from a video in another tab, or
  from a laptop that's been shut. Only closing the page stops a session there.
- **Leaving is reported by the page:** a modified client could stay quiet when
  it's hidden. As with proximity, the server trusts friends' apps here.
- **Cheating:** rewards are computed in Postgres from server time, so a client
  can't award itself chips. Chat is friends-only and unstored, so there's
  nothing to moderate after the fact, but also no history.
- **Scale:** each open client polls every 10 s, which is fine for tens of
  players. Move to Realtime Postgres Changes if it grows.
- **Realtime volume:** a walking player sends about 5 position messages a
  second, and each is delivered to every friend listening. That's comfortable
  for a friend group on Supabase's free Realtime quotas, but worth watching if
  the group grows.
- **Proximity is client-side:** the server checks that buyer and seller are
  friends and both on a break, not that they're standing together. A modified
  client could buy from a friend's stall from across the garden (but only at
  the seller's price).
